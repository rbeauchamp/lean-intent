# ADR-0005: Intent records, refinement transfer limits, answers independence, and cone examples

## Status

Accepted

## Context

Core modules (`LeanIntent/Record.lean`, `LeanIntent/Obligations.lean`, `LeanIntent/Refinement.lean`, `LeanIntent/Answers.lean`, `LeanIntent/Explain.lean`, `LeanIntent/Generic.lean`, `LeanIntent/Progress.lean`, `LeanIntent/Confirmation/Read.lean`) and worked examples (`LeanIntentExamples/Sorting.lean`, `LeanIntentExamples/Transfer.lean`, `LeanIntentExamples/Answers.lean`, `LeanIntentExamples/Cone.lean`) held non-obvious semantic and specification invariants in comments removed in Wave 1 and `#101` that go beyond what their Lean types alone display.

## Decision

### Intent records (`LeanIntent/Record.lean`)

- **Domain hypotheses vs `Meets`/`Fulfilled`**: Subject-independent world facts are encoded as constant `W → Prop` clauses in `IntentRecord.dom` so every relied-on world fact appears in the owner's restatement (and agent-added ones appear as decisions on the owner's behalf). `Fulfilled` is defined as `r.Dom w → r.Props w` rather than the conjunction `Meets` (`r.Dom w ∧ r.Props w`), because an implementation can only prove its properties under the world's domain hypotheses — it cannot establish facts about the external world.
- **Multi-argument functions in `Shape.bundle` (`Member`)**: Each `Member` is unary (`In → Out`). Multi-argument functions pack arguments together in `In` or leave trailing arguments in `Out`; that choice determines whether `Member.Ignores` under obligation O4 checks ignoring all arguments together or ignoring the first argument.
- **Independent specification of `Obligations.intendedO1`–`intendedO5` and `intendedO2Run` (`LeanIntent/Obligations.lean`)**: The five propositional obligation correctness theorems (`o2_correct`, `o2Run_correct`, `o3_correct`, `o4_correct`, `o5_correct`) equate (`↔`) each obligation proposition (`O2`, `O2Run`, `O3`, `O4`, `O5`) with an intended proposition (`intendedO2`, `intendedO2Run`, `intendedO3`, `intendedO4`, `intendedO5`); O1 instead returns a list of typed statements (`List ((T : Type u) × T)`), where `o1_correct` (`LeanIntent/Obligations.lean:38`–`44`) proves list equality `o1 r = intendedO1 r` pairing each domain clause, property clause, and vocabulary predicate with the type it is admitted at (`⟨W → Prop, c.holds⟩` or `⟨p.Arg → Prop, p.holds⟩`) and `o1_wellTyped` (`LeanIntent/Obligations.lean:46`–`47`) separately establishes construction-time well-typedness (`∀ g ∈ o1 r, Nonempty g.1`) checked by Lean's kernel when the record is declared. All six intended specifications are written directly by pattern matching on the record without reusing any of the 16 former/helper definitions (`Goals`, `HasInstance`, `HasNonInstance`, `Clause.typed`, `Pred.typed`, `IntentRecord.Dom`, `IntentRecord.Props`, `IntentRecord.Meets`, `Member.Ignores`, `Shape.Covers`, `Shape.Free`, `Shape.Sound`, `Shape.Idle`, `Shape.progress`, `Total`, `Freedom.relates`), so a defect in a helper cannot pass silently through both sides.

### Refinement (`LeanIntent/Refinement.lean`, `LeanIntentExamples/Sorting.lean`, `LeanIntentExamples/Transfer.lean`)

- **Domain hypotheses come only from the record**: A refinement link never introduces domain hypotheses of its own; every world fact the deliverable relies on must remain visible in the intent record.
- **Transfer limits**: `Refines` carries guarantees only to the mapping's image, not to the refining implementations themselves — specifically, O3 (refutability) and O5 `.eq` (determinacy) at the intent record do not make a refining specification refutable or determinate when no counterexample to the record lies in that image (`LeanIntentExamples/Transfer.lean`).
- **Implementation specs below the intent record**: While an intent record must be independent of implementation code, a lower-level implementation specification (such as `ImplSpec` in `LeanIntentExamples/Sorting.lean`, stating `∀ l, f l = l.mergeSort le`) may harmlessly name the implementation because its refinement link to the independent intent record (`implSpec_refines`) is machine-checked.

### Answers and undeclared claims (`LeanIntent/Answers.lean`, `LeanIntent/Confirmation/Read.lean`, `LeanIntentExamples/Answers.lean`)

- **Independent specification of `intendedKeeps`**: `keeps_correct` equates `Keeps r answers` with `intendedKeeps`, written by direct pattern matching over `answers` and `r` without reusing any of the six former/helper definitions (`Goals`, `HasInstance`, `Answer.KeptBy`, `IntentRecord.Meets`, `IntentRecord.Dom`, `IntentRecord.Props`), so a bug shared across helpers cannot slip past `keeps_correct`.
- **Self-referential predicate pitfall**: Because a stored `Answer` predicate is a Lean term elaborated in the revision's environment, editing a project definition the predicate names alters the stored answer's meaning without removing it. In particular a confirmed entailment stated through the record it answers (such as `fun f => sorting.Props f`) is kept by every revision made by changing the definition `sorting` itself, whatever the revision changes, because the predicate changes with it. That holds only when the named definition is the one revised: where `sorting` stays fixed and a separate record is checked as the revision, the predicate still means the original properties, and `sortedOnly` does not keep it, since `nilSort` meets `sortedOnly` (`sortedOnly_meets_nilSort`) and not `sorting` (`sorting_refutes_nilSort`, in `LeanIntentExamples/Sorting.lean`). Worked examples (`LeanIntentExamples/Answers.lean`) therefore restrict stored answer predicates (`emptyForEvery`, `rulesOutShort`) to Lean's own standard library so project edits cannot drift their meaning.
- **Undeclared confirmation claims (`Confirmation.Read.undeclaredIn`, #86)**: `undeclaredIn` enumerates the constants the given modules declare whose type unfolds via `Render.withExplanationTelescope` / `Render.isExplanationType` to `∀ ..., Explanation _`, `Attributed _` or a list of attributed answers, sharing the same kernel-WHNF (`Render.readWhnf`) + telescope shape definition as `Render.renderExplanation` (`#render_intent`, `#render_intent_exact`) and `Render.renderAt` (`#render_intent_at`, which enforces `isExplanationType` and `withExplanationTelescope` before specialization so conditional types such as `if b then Explanation r else Unit` cannot be specialized past the enumeration) so `explanationClaims` instantiates every binder of a generic explanation (such as `DayWorld.dayCycleExplained`) before inspecting `Explanation.mk`, and names each explanation among them that records a settled or confirmed claim and is no `Deliverable`'s, and each attributed answer, or list of them, holding one that no deliverable keeps. When the project has exactly one owners' log (`projectLog` succeeds), `Confirmation.Read.check` invokes `undeclaredIn` and reports a finding for each, so no declaration that `undeclaredIn` enumerates prints confirmation words the check never asked about (when there is no log or several, `check` returns the log findings immediately and still refuses the project). Its scope is that enumeration of declarations: a term given directly to `LeanIntent.Render.answerText`, which takes an in-memory `Expr` and formats its attribution, is no declaration and is outside it.
- **Typed restatement separation and unambiguous consequence selection (`Confirmation.Target`, `Confirmation.Asked`, `Confirmation.buildAnswer`, `Render.consequenceTextVerified`, #92)**: The revision's plain restatement is modeled by `Target.restatement` / `Asked.restatement` rather than a `Question` value, so no consequence `Question` can collide with or be shadowed by `restatementConsequence` (`"the record's plain restatement"`). `isRestatementAnswer` checks all three restatement sentinel fields (`consequence == restatementConsequence`, `kind == .entailment`, `predicate == restatementPredicate`), `Question.names` requires `!isRestatementAnswer a` (`Target.names_disjoint`), `Render.consequenceTextVerified` preserves the prefix invariant `Render.consequenceBody_prefix` for `Render.consequenceHeading`, `Confirmation.Read.consequenceBody_ne_restatementPredicate` and `questionOfVerified` / `revisionOfVerified` prove every consequence predicate produced by `Render.consequenceText` is distinct from `restatementPredicate`, and `Confirmation.buildAnswer` (called by `Read.answerStatement`) selects `.consequence name` by matching `(rev.shown.map (·.1)).filter (·.consequence == name)` against `[q]`—rejecting both missing (`[]`) and ambiguous duplicate (`_ :: _ :: _`) consequence names across the full revision—and enforces `!isRestatementAnswer a` at the builder boundary so every successful consequence answer identifies a unique shown question in `rev.shown` and preserves all fields (`buildAnswer_consequence`), any name shared by two or more shown questions is rejected (`buildAnswer_consequence_ambiguous`), and every uniquely named shown consequence whose predicate differs from `restatementPredicate` produces a non-restatement answer (`buildAnswer_consequence_not_restatement`, `buildAnswer_consequence_of_mem`).
- **Module ownership and `declaredWhere` scanning (`LeanIntent/Confirmation/Read.lean`)**: `moduleName?` reads imported module names directly from `header.modules[i]?.map (·.module)` because in Lean v4.36.0's `src/Lean/Environment.lean` (line 165), `header.moduleNames` is initialized to `modules.map (·.module)`. `declaredWhere` attributes a constant to the module recorded in `Environment.const2ModIdx` (or the current module when none is recorded): Lean v4.36.0 records module ownership only from `constNames` and the `extraConstNames` of the data read as the module's IR (`finalizeImport`, `src/Lean/Environment.lean`, lines 2697–2712), which is the module's own data or its `.ir` whose `extraConstNames` are its stored IR declaration names (`ImportedModule.irData?`, lines 2410–2418; `mkIRData`, lines 2233–2241; `src/LeanIR.lean`, lines 41–57); thus `declaredWhere` scans `data.constNames`, `data.extraConstNames`, and `IR.declMapExt.getModuleIREntries` names kept once where `const2ModIdx` records that module, and an environment reading a module's `.ir.sig` as its IR (made only by Lean's `leanir`) is outside this account.

### Cone exclusion non-vacuity and module-file `import all` (`LeanIntentExamples/Cone.lean`)

- Because no Lean theorem can name Lean's live `Environment`, `LeanIntent.Cone`'s theorems quantify over arbitrary `Name → Option ConstantInfo` lookups, and `LeanIntentExamples/Cone.lean` builds a hand-written lookup to prove non-vacuity on concrete cheat/honest records.
- The four `import all` imports in `LeanIntentExamples/Cone.lean` correspond to four functions Lean v4.36.0 does not expose to `module` files: `Init.Prelude` (`Lean.Name.beq`), `Lean.Data.Name` (`Lean.Name.isPrefixOf`), `Lean.PrivateName` (`Lean.privateToUserName`), and `Lean.Declaration` (`Lean.ConstantInfo.isAxiom`).

## Consequences

- In Wave 1, `Record.lean`, `Refinement.lean`, `Answers.lean`, `Explain.lean`, `Generic.lean`, `Progress.lean`, and `LeanIntentExamples/` (`Sorting.lean`, `Transfer.lean`, `Answers.lean`, `Cone.lean`) were cleared to zero comments and `undeclaredIn`'s docstring was removed from `Confirmation/Read.lean`; for #101, `LeanIntent/Confirmation/Read.lean` (together with `Obligations.lean`, `Cone.lean`, `Confirmation.lean`, `Settled.lean`, `Statement.lean`, `Trace.lean`, and every remaining module in `tools/comment-ratchet.json`) is cleared to zero comments, with each invariant above documented here beside `docs/intent-record.md`, `docs/refinement.md`, `docs/answers.md`, `docs/confirmation.md`, `docs/owners-log.md`, and `docs/cone-exclusion.md`.
